How I increased activation by 17% and D7 retention from 21% to 40%.

My goal was to understand why users were ignoring content and improve discovery without creating visual chaos.

Step 01

Users logged in — but left before finding value.

Context

We saw a high drop-off shortly after users entered the platform.

The problem was not that users could not access content. They could. But after logging in, they were immediately shown a large feed of videos with no guidance and no personalization.

From user interviews, I learned that people did not clearly understand where to start or which content was relevant to them. The platform had a lot to offer, but the first experience felt like a wall of content.

How I worked
→ Focused on the first moments after sign-up.
→ Added a short onboarding flow that asked users about their interests and preferences.
→ Instead of showing the same feed to everyone, the platform used these answers to make the first content recommendations more relevant.
→
Outcome & I learned

The onboarding became the first step in personalization. Instead of asking users to explore a large library on their own, the product started guiding them from the beginning.

The first screen should not show everything the product has. It should help the user understand what is useful for them first.

Step 02

Search existed — but users could not find it.

Context

Users wanted to find specific videos, but the search was too easy to miss.

Instead of using search, they were manually scrolling through the platform and looking through many videos one by one.

This made discovery slower and increased the chance that users would leave before finding something relevant.

How I worked
→ Made the search panel much more visible and gave it stronger visual priority on the page.
→ Added the most popular categories next to search — giving users two ways to move forward: search for something specific, or explore popular topics without knowing what to type.
→ The goal was to reduce the effort needed to find useful content and give users a clear next step.
→
Outcome & I learned

Search became a stronger part of the discovery experience. Users no longer had to rely only on manual browsing.

Popular categories also helped reduce the "empty search" problem by giving users ideas of what to explore.

Search only works if users can actually notice it. Sometimes the problem is not the feature — it is its visibility.

Step 03

Banner blindness: users saw the homepage, but remembered nothing.

Context

The homepage had a lot of videos, but most of them looked very similar.

I ran a 5-second test with the old design. After seeing the homepage for five seconds, users could not remember even one specific video.

This showed a banner blindness problem — there was a lot of content on screen, but nothing stood out enough to be remembered.

How I worked
→ Explored how to break the visual repetition without making the homepage feel chaotic.
→ Tested different card sizes, shapes, layouts, and content blocks to create stronger visual hierarchy.
→ The goal was to make important content easier to notice and help users scan the page faster.
→
Outcome & I learned

We didn't need more content. We needed to make the existing content easier to notice.

Instead of giving every video the same visual weight, I created more contrast between content blocks.

Consistency does not mean making everything look the same. A good system needs enough variation to guide attention.

A scalable system that made content easier to notice and understand.

Final impact

The redesign moved the homepage away from one repeated visual pattern and towards a system where different types of content could stand out while still feeling consistent.

The wider product improvements contributed to a 17% increase in activation and improved D7 retention from 21% to 40%.

The new content system also gave the team a scalable way to work with thousands of videos instead of treating every thumbnail as a separate design task.

The biggest lesson

Consistency and repetition are not the same thing. A product can stay consistent without making every piece of content look identical.

The design system should create enough structure to keep the experience clear and enough variation to help users notice what matters.
